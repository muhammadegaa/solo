Three looks for the check-in call

Each direction shows the same three screens at real iPhone size: the AI speaking with last week's sleep, you speaking while today's plan changes, and the /dashboard on the phone. The words and example data are the same, so only the identity changes.

Direction A

First light

The call happens at dawn. The screen is an early-morning sky, the AI's voice is a thin horizon line that moves as it speaks, and captions are set large in a book serif, like subtitles. It is calm and slow, made for 07:30 before the house wakes up.

Direction B

Explorer map

Built from a UK walking map: a blue grid, contour lines and legend boxes with black rules. The AI's voice shows as contour rings around a summit marker. It puts the main action, getting outside, at the centre of the design.

Direction C

Dictaphone

A small physical recorder: brushed metal, a green LCD showing the call timer and voice level, and chunky keys that press in. It feels like a tool you pick up, talk into and put down, which suits a short daily habit.

How they compare

  • First light is the calmest and reads best at 07:30. The large serif captions make it the easiest to follow while half awake. It relies on a background gradient, which needs care so it doesn't look generic.
  • Explorer map is the most distinctive and the most UK-specific, and it keeps walking at the centre. The square map styling looks less like an app and more like print, which some people find colder.
  • Dictaphone feels the most physical and works well for a short daily habit. Its device styling (metal, LCD, keys) is the hardest to build well and could look dated.

All figures are examples, not your Polar data.
